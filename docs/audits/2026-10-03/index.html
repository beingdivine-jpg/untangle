The main conclusion

I would not yet recommend this version for an unassisted rollout to people with very little digital confidence. Ordinary navigation can erase a plan, later drafts replace earlier work, a valid setup path produces no explanation, and the advertised AI is unavailable on the live deployment.

The product has a useful role as a guided planning companion. Retain its warm visual identity, sourced explanations, uncertainty-aware outcomes and permission boundaries. More features will help after the current journey becomes coherent.

Keep these strengths

  • No account connection is required; local planning works without AI.
  • Fictional examples are labelled and changes are not secretly applied.
  • “Reviewed” is separated from “safe”; uncertainty remains visible.
  • Official guidance and limitations are linked.
  • Notes are hidden by default in the together view.
  • Optional encrypted files and explicit AI consent are implemented.
  • The visible English/Polski control, logo and warm identity are worth keeping.

What the audit establishes

A live cognitive walkthrough, UI interactions using fictional data, source review, public response inspection and automated tests.

The beginner perspective is simulated: a young person on a phone, upset after a breakup, unfamiliar with security terms, recovery settings and file management. It is not evidence from observing actual teenagers or representative users.

No real accounts were connected, no real personal notes submitted and no account settings changed. Product behavior was not modified in this audit.

Is the visitor logged in?

No. Untangle has no user accounts or login session. The interface becomes personal because it holds selections in the open tab. It does not establish identity, connect Google/Apple/WhatsApp or keep a cloud copy.

P1 F01 · Reproduced live · Effort: Medium

Returning to the introduction silently destroys the full plan

Evidence

Created a plan, recorded a fictional uncertain outcome and note, selected Back to introduction, then Already have a plan? Open the tools. The workspace reopened empty. Reproduced again with a four-check plan; before/after screenshots are included. App.tsx conditionally unmounts LegacyApp and clears its seed on this navigation.

Reproduce or inspect

Start with my own situation → Build a draft on this device → Keep these steps → Open my full plan → optionally record an update → Back to introduction → Already have a plan? Open the tools.

Why it matters to a beginner

A beginner reasonably treats introduction/home as navigation. Losing work after explicitly keeping an update damages trust and requires repeating emotionally difficult work.

Recommended change

Keep the current plan in one application-level, memory-only store across ordinary internal navigation. Offer Resume this plan from the introduction. Reserve clearing for explicit clear/exit and the documented document-lifecycle privacy boundary. Preserve a separate fictional demo state.

Acceptance check

Checks, outcomes, notes and history survive introduction → resume in both languages. Quick exit, explicit clear and refresh still clear according to the chosen privacy policy. Add this complete journey to regression coverage.

P1 F02 · Reproduced live · Effort: Medium

Keeping another draft replaces previously kept checks without explaining it

Evidence

Kept the default six accounts checks. Deselected Accounts & messages, selected Location sharing, built a new draft and kept it. The result became two checks: Maps and iPhone. The earlier Google and WhatsApp checks disappeared. While changing topics, the old kept list and the new topic picture also disagreed.

Reproduce or inspect

Keep an accounts draft → change the selected topic to location → build and keep the new draft → inspect the kept count and full plan.

Why it matters to a beginner

A person working through several concerns can unknowingly erase earlier decisions. The label Keep these steps suggests retaining work, without indicating replacement.

Recommended change

Make adding the default: merge new checks with kept checks and retain their notes/status. If replacement is useful, provide a separate Replace plan action with a preview of removed items. Keep a clear Undo for this reversible operation.

Acceptance check

Adding a location draft to the accounts plan keeps the union of checks without duplicates or lost notes. Replacement names what will be removed and requires an explicit choice.

P1 F03 · Reproduced live · Effort: Small

A valid onboarding choice can return an unexplained empty result

Evidence

In the full-plan setup, selected Can someone see where I am?, then only WhatsApp, then Make my plan. The app returned to the welcome screen with Make my own plan, without explaining that there was no matching guide. buildPlan removes both location guides for that service selection; hasPlan then evaluates false.

Reproduce or inspect

Open the tools → Make my own plan → location concern → Continue → WhatsApp only → Make my plan.

Why it matters to a beginner

The user has done what the website asked and appears to be sent back to the start. She cannot distinguish unsupported coverage from her own mistake or a broken submission.

Recommended change

Treat no matching guide as a real outcome. Keep the concern, explain the coverage gap in plain language, and offer relevant reviewed external help or a question to take to a trusted person. Let the user change the app choice without restarting.

Acceptance check

Exercise every concern/service combination. None silently becomes the generic welcome screen. Unsupported combinations retain the user's concern and present a useful next action.

P2 F04 · Reproduced live · Effort: Medium

Uncommitted notes and outcome selections disappear during ordinary navigation

Evidence

Typed a fictional reminder and selected I'm not sure what I found in a Maps guide. Clicked My plan before Keep this update. Reopening the guide's My update section showed an empty note, no selected outcome and a disabled submit button. There was no pending-change indication or warning.

Reproduce or inspect

Open a check → My update → type a note and choose an outcome → My plan → reopen the same check → My update.

Why it matters to a beginner

The difference between typing, choosing and keeping is easy to miss. A distracted visitor may think the app has kept the note because the rest of the interface updates instantly.

Recommended change

Retain draft notes in the memory-only plan store across internal navigation. Clearly distinguish a draft update from a recorded update. If discarding, offer Keep editing or Discard, without obstructing quick exit.

Acceptance check

A draft survives navigation to guides, support and the plan. Explicit discard clears it. Quick exit remains immediate. Editing a previously recorded note makes its current outcome understandable.

P2 F05 · Reproduced in an isolated code probe · Effort: Small

An allowed large Unicode plan can be exported but cannot be reopened

Evidence

A valid plan containing all 13 checks, 30 history entries each and 500-character three-byte Unicode notes exports successfully to 838,863 bytes. Its ciphertext is 838,688 base64 characters, above decode's 750,000-character cap. Reopening with the correct passphrase fails with the generic passphrase/file error, despite being below the 1 MB file limit. This is an edge case, not a claim that typical English or Polish plans fail.

Reproduce or inspect

Use the included maximum-plan.test.ts.txt probe with the project's Vitest runner. It calls the actual validators and encryption/decryption functions; its round-trip assertion fails.

Why it matters to a beginner

A user can be told her private copy was downloaded, close the tab, and later discover that her only saved copy is unusable. The error incorrectly points her toward the passphrase or file corruption.

Recommended change

Define one byte budget shared by validation, export and import. Ensure every successfully exported plan is importable. Account for UTF-8, JSON escaping and base64 expansion, or reject the export clearly before generating a file.

Acceptance check

Round-trip maximum supported histories containing ASCII, Polish letters, three-byte characters, emoji and escaped characters. Every accepted export reopens; any unsupported size fails before download with an accurate message.

P2 F06 · Live behavior and product assessment · Effort: Small for honest fallback; Large for production AI

The main AI promise leads to a disabled primary action on production

Evidence

The introduction promotes AI-assisted drafts. The live status endpoint returns available:false. The personal workspace displays a disabled Ask AI for a draft as its primary button; the working local planner is secondary. AI rewriting is also unavailable for personal notes; manual note editing still works. Try Me is correctly labelled as scripted, and the local planner works.

Reproduce or inspect

Open the introduction → Start with my own situation → inspect Ask AI for a draft and Help me put it into words. Compare with /api/agent/status.

Why it matters to a beginner

A visitor attracted by the headline benefit encounters a feature she cannot use. Installation/configuration language makes a deployment condition feel like something the user must solve.

Recommended change

While AI is off, make Show my first steps the primary working action and label the companion as guided planning. Keep the AI demonstration clearly separate. Before enabling real AI, build a production server route, server-side secret management, abuse/cost controls and failure handling; the current adapter is loopback-only.

Acceptance check

Production always has a usable primary action. Claims match the actual mode. No dead-end editor is presented as available. A real AI release proves consent, cancellation, timeout, malformed-output handling and spending controls end to end.

P2 F07 · Source-confirmed behavior; novice-usability judgment · Effort: Small

Account and saving state are accurate in help, but insufficiently visible in the journey

Evidence

There is no Untangle login, identity session, cloud account or automatic saving. Work exists in React memory; a downloaded encrypted file is the only durable plan. Yet Your workspace, Your personal plan, Keep these steps and Keep my place can sound persistent. The explicit No account to create sidebar note is hidden on mobile, and the detailed lifecycle explanation sits in help/disclosures or Save & resume.

Reproduce or inspect

Arrive on a phone and try to answer: Am I signed in? Who can see this? Will it be here tomorrow? Where would I sign back in? The product's answers are distributed across multiple screens.

Why it matters to a beginner

This is especially important for a person who equates a personal-looking page with an account. Absence of login is a useful privacy choice; ambiguity about it is the problem.

Recommended change

Use a persistent plain state label such as This tab only · Not saved to a file. Explain before the first personal input: You don't need an Untangle account. Refreshing or closing this page clears this session. Change the badge after download and after subsequent edits. Keep Google/Apple sign-in explicitly separate from Untangle.

Acceptance check

In an unprompted comprehension test, participants can explain no account, no automatic cloud saving and how to return later. This information remains available on mobile and in Polish without opening a long privacy panel.

Evidence

P2 F12 · Source-confirmed UI gap · Effort: Medium

People can add checks but cannot easily correct the plan's scope

Evidence

The full plan supports Add a check, review outcomes and Clear plan. It has no per-check Remove / Doesn't apply action, no editable service selection after setup and no way to correct a mistaken scope without rebuilding or tolerating irrelevant rows. P2 F13 · Observed guide content; usability judgment · Effort: Large

Most guides stop short of helping someone who cannot find the setting

Evidence

Most of the 13 guides have two text instructions plus an external help link. The Maps guide says to find Location sharing in a profile menu. Gmail forwarding and Google Photos instructions require a computer. A slower, contextual walkthrough with I'm stuck exists for Google devices, but the other guides do not provide equivalent help. Google's linked Maps page begins with how to share location before its stop-sharing section.

Reproduce or inspect

Try the Maps, forwarding, Photos and WhatsApp guides from a phone without prior knowledge of profile menus, settings or switching between tabs. Compare them with the slower device walkthrough.

Why it matters to a beginner

A person can reach the correct guide and still have no idea what to tap, which account is open, or how to return. Sending her into long provider documentation moves the complexity elsewhere.

Recommended change

Ask phone/computer only when it matters. Show one small instruction at a time with a maintained, clearly illustrative example, I can't find this, My screen looks different and I'll ask someone options. Label external account pages and explain returning to Untangle. Include supervised-account and age/region limitations where relevant, without collecting unnecessary birth-date data.

Acceptance check

First-time users can locate a setting or reach an appropriate help path without guessing. Phone users are never led into a computer-only instruction without explanation. Provider sign-in is never presented as an Untangle login.

P2 F14 · Observed UI; return-use hypothesis · Effort: Medium

Returning another day depends on a file workflow that may exceed the audience's skills

Evidence

Save & resume requires a new file passphrase of at least 12 characters, entered twice, a download, later finding a .untangle file, entering its passphrase and explicitly applying the import. There is no passphrase reveal control, download-location tutorial or restore rehearsal. The app cannot verify that the browser actually saved the file; it announces download after invoking the link.

Reproduce or inspect

Read the Save & resume flow as someone unfamiliar with Downloads, file extensions or the difference between an account password and a file passphrase. Existing tests verify encryption and import mechanics, not novice success.

Why it matters to a beginner

The product's repeat-use promise is fragile for this audience. A successful click does not demonstrate that the person can recover the plan later.

Recommended change

Keep the optional encrypted file, but guide the user through Save for later, what file to look for, a reveal/hide control, and an optional reopen check. Use Started download unless completion is knowable. Offer Keep using this tab with a clear temporary-state reminder. Do not introduce silent storage or cloud accounts just to solve this UX problem.

Acceptance check

At least four of five representative pilot participants can reopen a fictional plan on their own device in a later session. This is a proposed release target, not current adoption evidence.

P2 F15 · Measured live; visual review · Effort: Medium

The mobile page fits, but small text and action depth undermine simplicity

Evidence

At 390×844, the personal workspace's working Build a draft on this device action began at approximately y=1,065 CSS pixels, below the first viewport. The disabled AI button was above it. Full-plan mobile navigation text measured 10px; diagram helper labels were 9px; some important explanations were 12px. The first landing-page Try Me button was visible at y=514–568, and horizontal overflow was not observed.

Reproduce or inspect

Use a 390×844 viewport, enter the personal workspace and inspect the first screen and computed text sizes. Compare with Polish navigation wrapping.

Why it matters to a beginner

The visitor must scroll through topic controls, tool tabs and introductory text before finding a working next step. Large visual headings coexist with tiny practical navigation.

Recommended change

Move the next action directly below the relevant choice; progressively reveal optional tools. Aim for 16px reading text and comfortably readable navigation, with approximately 44px touch controls as a design target. Preserve the noticed English/Polski switch. Test text enlargement and actual phone keyboards.

Acceptance check

At 320–390px widths, the primary action is easy to discover without scanning multiple tool sections. Enlarged text does not hide controls or force horizontal reading. Small font sizes alone are not being labelled a WCAG violation; target spacing and exceptions need separate assessment.

P2 F16 · Observed information architecture; usability judgment · Effort: Medium

Human and age-appropriate support is useful but too far from the first decision

Evidence

The companion's human-support link is at the bottom and opens Lila.help. The full plan has richer support, including a Poland selector, youth support and private-image guidance. The introduction exposes help through an icon, while the default Photos topic selects preservation and partner-sharing checks rather than an obvious urgent-image-support branch. The UI correctly states that Untangle is not a live or emergency service.

Reproduce or inspect

From the first mobile screen, try to find help for being unable to continue, concerns about changing access, or private images. Compare the effort with opening the full support page.

Why it matters to a beginner

Someone overwhelmed or dealing with threats should not need to learn the planning workspace to reach appropriate human help. A youth-oriented support option also should not be the only clearly regional route for an adult visitor.

Recommended change

Add a consistently placed Find someone to help link and a clearly optional I don't feel comfortable checking route. Ask region only when support is requested; do not infer it from language. Present youth and adult options clearly and surface private-image support by concern.

Acceptance check

Relevant human support can be reached in one or two clear actions from each main screen. The destination and whether it is a live service are explicit. The existing under-18 image guidance and no-upload boundary remain intact.

P2 F17 · Observed Polish UI; source-confirmed gaps · Effort: Medium

Polish UI coverage is strong, but the whole help journey is not consistently localized

Evidence

The language switch remains visible and preserves personal notes in existing tests. Google support links change hl=en to hl=pl. Apple en-euro and most other external support URLs remain unchanged; there is no consistent language notice before leaving. The fallback translator silently returns English when a key is missing. Reproduce or inspect

Switch to Polish, open Apple and non-Google guidance, and inspect localizedUrl. Compare page metadata and the translated UI. Treat naturalness/comprehension as a validation task, not a proven mistranslation.

Why it matters to a beginner

A Polish-speaking beginner can make progress and then unexpectedly enter English documentation. A future English copy edit may also silently break the corresponding translation key.

Recommended change

Use verified Polish provider links where available; otherwise label Opens an English-language guide and offer a Polish explanation. Use stable translation keys, development missing-key diagnostics and coverage checks. Commission a native Polish review of safety-sensitive and beginner-facing language.

Acceptance check

The top user journeys work end to end in Polish. Personal notes remain unchanged. Missing translations fail a build/test or are reported during development. External-language changes are communicated before the click.

A useful novice validation plan

Use a small, consented group with low digital confidence across English and Polish, fictional scenarios and real phones. Adults can establish the first usability baseline; youth-specific research requires appropriate specialist support and safeguards.

  1. Ask participants to explain what Untangle can do, whether they are logged in and whether the app can see their accounts.
  2. Give one concern without naming the correct button. Observe whether they find a relevant instruction and understand that no account setting has changed.
  3. Let them get stuck, seek help, switch language and correct a wrong choice. Record where assistance is needed.
  4. Record uncertainty, visit the introduction and return. Ordinary internal navigation should never silently lose work.
  5. Save a fictional plan and return later. Observe whether the file can be found and reopened without coaching.

Proposed pilot targets: all participants understand the account/saving model after its explanation; at least four of five reach a relevant first check without coaching; at least four of five restore a fictional plan later; zero silent losses in scripted internal-navigation checks. These are proposed targets, not statistically representative adoption claims.

Record completion, time to first useful instruction, errors, help requests and later recovery with sample sizes. Avoid default recordings or sensitive free-text telemetry. Current adoption, retention and conversion rates are unknown.
