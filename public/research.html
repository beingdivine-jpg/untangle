Product research · 3 October 2026

A clearer next step, with the person in control.

People do not separate their digital lives in one sitting—or inside one app. Untangle should help them understand connections, preserve what matters, choose a next step, and return to what remains unresolved.

Desk research and a bounded competitor walkthrough. This is a product hypothesis, not evidence of user validation or a claim that competing products lack every feature described below.

1. The problem we are actually addressing

A breakup can leave shared logins, devices, recovery channels, location permissions, photo libraries and everyday services behind. For some people these are ordinary practical loose ends; for others, technology is part of coercive control. The product must help without assuming every unexplained setting is abuse or treating a breakup as proof of compromise.

Research on computer-security consultations with 44 survivors describes the need to work through accounts, devices and configurations in context. It supports an assisted, situation-specific approach; it does not validate this prototype. Account mapping and coordinated safety work have prior art. Havron et al., USENIX Security 2019.

Safety planning requires considering whether a change could be noticed, what access a person depends on, and when another person’s help would be useful. Removing access is not automatically the right first action. NNEDV Safety Net: technology safety planning.

The core job: “Help me work out what is still connected, what I might lose by changing it, and what I want to do next—without making me learn security terminology first.”

4. Innovativeness: an interaction to validate

Proposed position: a transparent planning companion for the digital ties after a breakup. The person sees the input, follows the work, explores consequences and approves the result. The existing knot logo and Untangle name make this understandable: the thread represents choices across services. A new name would not add functional differentiation.

5. “Predictive” needs a clear boundary

The implemented “What if I change…” tool explains conditional consequences from official guidance. It does not run a predictive model, estimate danger, attribute account activity to a person, or forecast an ex-partner’s response. The thread is an index of selected checks, not a discovered map of someone’s devices.

A password is one thread

A password change can end many sessions, with documented exceptions. It is not a review of forwarding, separate location settings or saved photo copies. Preparation and remaining checks stay visible.

A photo change has limits

Stopping partner sharing affects ongoing sharing; already saved copies remain. The interface surfaces what to keep before deciding. Opening this preview does not mark anything completed.

Source-specific explanations are listed below. AI does not generate the consequence text. This avoids presenting fluent speculation as an account finding; it does not make the static guidance universally applicable.

6. A useful difference to test

The strongest opportunity is continuity across services, with consequences made understandable. A person can already find good advice. The proposed value is keeping the advice connected to her situation: “What should I consider first?”, “What might this interrupt?”, “What did I actually find?”, and “What do I still want help with?”

No reviewed source establishes that this combination is globally unique. Competitor feature coverage was not exhaustively audited. We should describe this as Untangle’s product focus and test whether it helps people more than a good existing guide.

Keep the connection between tasks

Recovery access before account changes. Content to preserve before leaving shared spaces. A visible explanation for the suggested order, with freedom to read any guide.

Preserve uncertainty

“Still a question”, “for later” and “with someone’s help” remain open states. A reviewed check records activity; it is not proof that an account is safe.

Return without a new account

An optional encrypted plan file carries selected checks, notes and bounded update history between visits. No silent cloud sync, account connection or background notifications.

Make asking for help concrete

A review-together page gathers open checks, with notes hidden by default. The user chooses what to show and asks the helper to agree before changing settings.

8. What this version actually includes

  • A one-page introduction and three scripted Try Me stories, with a visual thread, a visible local activity record, draft selection, consequence previews and reversible wording edits.
  • An optional live OpenAI adapter for guide selection and note editing. A person reviews the exact fields and explicitly consents before each request; drafts require a second acceptance before changing the local plan or note. The current installation has no API key, so live inference has not been tested; the labelled local demonstration works independently.
  • A familiar workspace with a personal plan, searchable guides, review-together view, save/resume and regional support links.
  • Concern and service selection, preparation dependencies, one suggested next check, explicit outcome updates and up to 30 previous updates per check.
  • Thirteen focused guides spanning Google access/recovery/passwords/forwarding, Google Maps, Apple Safety Check, preserving content, Google Photos, WhatsApp, Apple family services, home devices, image-abuse support and asking for help.
  • Opt-in local-file encryption using Web Crypto AES-256-GCM and PBKDF2-SHA256 (310,000 iterations), random salt and IV, authenticated decryption, bounded file sizes and schema validation. These are implementation details, not an independent security certification.
  • A visibly fictional example plan. Earlier device walkthroughs and detailed review flows are preserved and accessible; their source snapshot is archived.

Live AI uses structured output and validates supported task IDs, scope and preparation. These controls do not prove an edited sentence preserves meaning or is appropriate; human review remains necessary. The local adapter has size/rate/time limits, cancellation, no provider tools and storage disabled. Storage disabled does not guarantee zero retention. The implementation is a loopback prototype, not a production service for minors. See structured-output documentation, provider data controls and under-18 guidance.

Provider-specific coverage remains intentionally limited. The guide library links to broader specialist resources for other apps, home devices and situations. A local plan file can be discovered, lost or become stale. It is not a cloud backup, evidence vault, spyware detector or verified record of account state.

9. What would prove this deserves to become a product?

The next research should involve practitioners and appropriately safeguarded sessions with the intended audience, including people with little digital experience. Do not recruit minors into sensitive research without appropriate safeguarding and consent arrangements. These are proposed research activities, not studies already performed.

  1. Compare with existing help. Ask participants to work through fictional cross-service scenarios using Untangle and an established guide. Counterbalance order; measure completion, uncertainty handling and comprehension.
  2. Test the critical misunderstandings. Can people explain that stopping photo sharing leaves saved copies, and that a reviewed check does not certify safety?
  3. Test continuity. Can someone return later, restore a plan, recognise stale observations, and find the next action without a researcher explaining it?
  4. Test supported use. Can a participant show only the notes they intend, identify a question for a helper, and retain control over proposed changes?
  5. Review the failure modes. Practitioner review of sequencing, content and referral choices; independent review of file encryption and lifecycle clearing; accessibility testing beyond automated checks.

Proposed pilot gates, not achieved results: at least 8 of 10 participants can explain what the assistant can and cannot do without prompting; at least 8 of 10 can find a relevant next step and distinguish preview from action; no unexpected transmission or silent draft application in any observed session. Test rejected edits and resumed plans as well as successful flows. A small pilot is exploratory, not proof of safety or a population estimate.

Report task success, time, help requests, misunderstandings and qualitative reactions. Do not equate time in app, number of checks completed or a self-reported confidence score with reduced abuse or improved security.

10. Hackathon fit and honest presentation

The supplied ImpactHer brief weights idea/innovation (30%), category relevance (20%), practical usability (20%), design (20%) and implementation (10%). A coherent cross-service workflow, demonstrable repeat use and clear novice comprehension are stronger evidence than adding more disconnected features.

The pitch should show a concrete multi-step example: Maya checks recovery access, cannot identify a laptop, learns what a password change will not address, keeps location sharing for a supported conversation, and returns to the same plan. Demonstrate real state changes and file restoration, while labelling all account observations as fictional or user-reported.

Disclose significant AI assistance, libraries and research sources, and distinguish pre-existing work from work completed during the hackathon. The supplied documents name different submission platforms; confirm the correct platform with the organiser. This implementation does not establish eligibility or make a submission.
